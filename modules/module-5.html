Connecting with People

Email, video calls, and messaging — technology makes it easier than ever to stay close to the people you love.

🟡 Intermediate ⏱ 13 minutes 📖 Reading

What You'll Learn

  • How email works and how to send your first message
  • Making video calls with Zoom, WhatsApp, and FaceTime
  • Text messaging and WhatsApp for quick messages
  • An overview of social media and keeping in touch with groups

Email — The Digital Letter

Email (short for "electronic mail") is like sending a letter — but it arrives in seconds and costs nothing. You can attach photos, documents, or other files along with your message.

To use email, you need an email address — a unique identifier that looks like yourname@gmail.com. Popular email services (all free) include Gmail (Google), Outlook (Microsoft), and Apple Mail.

📮

Email Is Like the Post Office

Your email address is like your home address — it's where people can send messages to you. Your "inbox" is your letterbox. Your "sent" folder is like keeping a copy of every letter you've posted. "Drafts" are letters you've started but not sent yet.

Key Parts of Your Email Account

  • Inbox — all the messages people have sent to you
  • Sent — copies of every message you've sent
  • Drafts — messages you've started but not yet sent
  • Spam / Junk — unwanted messages that have been filtered out automatically
  • Trash / Deleted — messages you've deleted (they stay here for a while before being permanently removed)

Sending an Email — Step by Step

1

Click "Compose" or "New Message"

This opens a blank email form, usually with boxes labelled "To," "Subject," and the message area.

2

Enter the recipient's address in "To:"

Type the email address of the person you're writing to. Be exact — one wrong letter and it won't arrive.

3

Add a Subject

A brief description of what the email is about. Like the subject line on a formal letter.

4

Write your message

Type in the main body area. You can be as formal or as casual as you like.

5

Click "Send"

Your message is sent instantly. It will appear in your "Sent" folder as confirmation.

💡

Attaching Photos

To send a photo in an email, look for a "paperclip" icon (📎) and click it. This lets you choose a photo from your device to attach to the email. The recipient will be able to open and save it on their device.

Video Calls — Seeing Each Other Face to Face

Video calling lets you see and hear someone in real time, no matter where they are in the world. For grandparents and grandchildren miles apart, this is one of technology's most wonderful gifts.

📹

FaceTime

Built into all Apple devices (iPhone, iPad, Mac). If both people have Apple devices, this is the simplest choice — just tap a contact's name.

🟢

WhatsApp

Free video and audio calls, works on all phones and computers. Great if you're not sure what type of device the other person has.

🔵

Zoom

Popular for group calls with multiple people. Excellent for family gatherings when everyone is in different locations.

🔷

Google Meet

Similar to Zoom. Works in a browser — no download needed. Good if you want simplicity.

💡

For the Best Video Call Experience

Good lighting makes a big difference — sit facing a window rather than having it behind you. Hold the tablet or phone steady, or prop it up. Use headphones or earphones if the sound echoes. Make sure you're on Wi-Fi rather than mobile data if possible.

WhatsApp and Text Messaging

Sometimes you want a quick message rather than a phone call. Text messaging (also called SMS) lets you send short written messages from one phone to another. It's been around since the 1990s and remains hugely popular.

WhatsApp is a free app that does the same thing but over Wi-Fi or mobile data — it's free to use anywhere in the world, unlike international text messages that can be expensive. WhatsApp lets you:

  • Send text messages for free
  • Send photos, videos, and voice notes
  • Make voice and video calls for free
  • Create "group chats" with multiple family members
  • Share your location if you need someone to find you
ℹ️

Group Family Chats

One of the most delightful things about WhatsApp is a family group chat. Everyone in the family joins, and you can all share news, photos, and jokes in one place. It brings families together in a warm, daily way — even when miles apart.

Social Media — A Brief Overview

Social media platforms let you connect with friends, family, groups, and communities online. You create a profile, and then you can follow or connect with people and organisations you're interested in. Here are the most well-known ones:

  • Facebook — the most widely used by the 60+ age group. Share updates, photos, and join community groups. Great for reconnecting with old friends.
  • Instagram — primarily for sharing photos and short videos. More popular with younger people but widely used.
  • YouTube — not traditional social media, but you can follow channels of interest (cooking, gardening, music, travel) and watch videos for free.
⚠️

A Word of Caution

Social media can be wonderful, but be mindful of what you share publicly (more on this in the Online Safety module). Check your privacy settings to control who sees your posts. And remember — not everything you read on social media is accurate or true. We'll cover this more in Module 6.

Key Takeaways

  • ✓ Email is a free, instant digital letter — great for longer messages and sending photos
  • ✓ Video calls (FaceTime, WhatsApp, Zoom) let you see and speak with loved ones anywhere in the world for free
  • ✓ WhatsApp is a versatile free app for texting, photos, voice notes, and calls worldwide
  • ✓ Family group chats are a lovely way to stay connected daily
  • ✓ Social media is useful for communities and reconnecting, but be mindful of what you share publicly
