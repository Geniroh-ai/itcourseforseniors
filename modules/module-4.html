Discovering the Internet

The world's biggest library, shop, and telephone exchange — all at your fingertips. Let's explore it safely.

🟢 Beginner ⏱ 13 minutes 📖 Reading

What You'll Learn

  • What the internet actually is — in simple, clear terms
  • What a web browser is and how to use one
  • How to read a web address (URL)
  • How to search for anything using a search engine
  • The difference between the internet and the World Wide Web

What Is the Internet?

The internet is a vast, worldwide network of computers that are connected to each other. When you connect to the internet, your computer (or phone) joins this enormous network and can communicate with computers all over the world.

Those other computers might belong to businesses, universities, governments, news organisations, or individuals — and they share information with your device through this network.

🛣️

Think of It as Roads

Imagine a country's road network. Roads connect towns and cities, allowing cars (information) to travel between them. The internet is the same idea — but instead of roads connecting towns, it's cables and wireless signals connecting computers worldwide. And instead of cars, it's information travelling at the speed of light.

How Does It Reach My Home?

Your home connects to the internet through an Internet Service Provider (ISP) — a company like BT, Virgin Media, Sky, or TalkTalk in the UK. They run cables to your home, and you plug in a small box called a router (sometimes called a "hub"). The router creates a wireless signal (Wi-Fi) in your home that your computer, tablet, or phone can connect to.

  • When you see "Wi-Fi" on your device, it means you're connected wirelessly to your home router
  • When you're outside home, you might connect to Wi-Fi in a café or use "mobile data" (4G/5G) from your phone provider

The World Wide Web vs. The Internet

Many people use these terms interchangeably, but they're slightly different things:

  • The Internet is the physical infrastructure — the cables, routers, and connections
  • The World Wide Web (the "Web") is the system of websites that sits on top of the internet — the pages you actually visit
📞

Another Way to Think of It

The internet is like the telephone network (all the cables and exchanges). The World Wide Web is like the conversations and services that happen over those phone lines. Email, video calls, and streaming music also use the internet — they're not the Web, but they travel over the same network.

Web Browsers — Your Window to the Web

To visit websites, you need a web browser. A browser is a program that lets you navigate the internet — it takes website addresses, contacts the right computer, and displays the page on your screen in a readable format.

🔵

Google Chrome

The most popular browser worldwide. Fast and works on all devices.

🟠

Firefox

A trusted, privacy-focused browser. Good choice for those who value security.

🔷

Microsoft Edge

Built into Windows computers. Good, fast, and easy to use.

🍎

Safari

Built into Apple devices (Mac, iPad, iPhone). Smooth and efficient.

They all do the same basic job. If you're on a Windows computer, Edge is already installed. On an Apple device, Safari is ready to go. All are free to use.

Parts of a Browser

When you open a browser, here are the key parts:

  • Address bar — the long box at the top where you type a website address. It usually shows the current page's address.
  • Back button (←) — goes back to the previous page you were on
  • Forward button (→) — goes forward if you've gone back
  • Refresh button (↻) — reloads the current page (useful if it didn't load properly)
  • Tabs — you can have multiple websites open at once in separate "tabs" at the top of the browser
  • Bookmarks — save favourite websites so you can return to them quickly

Understanding Web Addresses (URLs)

Every website has a unique address called a URL (Uniform Resource Locator). You type it into the address bar to go directly to that website. URLs look like this:

📍 Anatomy of a Web Address

https://www.bbc.co.uk/news

https

The Protocol

"https" means the connection is secure and encrypted. The padlock icon in your browser confirms this.

www

World Wide Web

Tells the system this is a webpage. Often not needed to type — your browser adds it automatically.

bbc

The Domain Name

The unique name of the website — in this case, the BBC.

.co.uk

The Extension

.co.uk = UK company. .com = typically international. .org = organisation. .gov = government.

💡

You Don't Need to Type the Whole Address

Most of the time, you can just type the main name into the address bar — like "bbc.co.uk" or even just "bbc" — and the browser will work it out. You don't need to type "https://www" manually.

Searching the Internet

You don't need to know a website address to find information. Search engines let you type any question or topic and will find relevant websites for you. The most popular search engine in the world is Google.

How to Search

1

Open your browser

Click or tap your browser icon to open it.

2

Click the address bar

Click in the long bar at the top. Type your search question — for example: "best parks in London" or "how to make Victoria sponge."

3

Press Enter

Google (or your default search engine) will show you a list of relevant results.

4

Click a result

Click on any blue link to visit that page. Press the back button to return to search results.

Tips for Better Searching

  • Use natural language — search as if you're asking a person: "what time does the post office open?"
  • Be specific — "Italian restaurants in Edinburgh" gives better results than just "restaurants"
  • Try different words if the first search doesn't give what you need
  • Look at results from well-known websites (NHS, BBC, gov.uk) for reliable information
⚠️

Watch Out for Advertisements

The first few results on Google are often paid advertisements — they look like normal results but have a small "Ad" or "Sponsored" label. These aren't necessarily bad, but the best results are usually the first non-advertisement ones below them.

Key Takeaways

  • ✓ The internet is a global network of connected computers; the Web is the system of websites on top of it
  • ✓ A web browser (Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Safari) is the program you use to visit websites
  • ✓ Web addresses (URLs) contain the site name — you usually only need to type the main part
  • ✓ Search engines like Google let you find anything by typing a question or topic
  • ✓ "https" and the padlock symbol mean a website connection is secure
