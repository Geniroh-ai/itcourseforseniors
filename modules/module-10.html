AI Assistants Around You

Siri, Alexa, Google — these friendly voices are powered by AI. Here's how they work and how to get the most from them.

What You'll Learn

  • What voice assistants are and how they understand you
  • The main voice assistants: Siri, Alexa, and Google Assistant
  • Practical commands to use right away
  • Smart speakers and what they can do at home
  • Privacy considerations with always-listening devices

What Is a Voice Assistant?

A voice assistant is an AI system that listens to your spoken words, understands what you're asking, and responds helpfully — either in speech, on screen, or by performing an action.

You've likely already encountered them: "Hey Siri," "OK Google," or "Alexa." You speak naturally, as if to another person, and the assistant tries to understand and help you.

📞

Like Having an Expert PA

Imagine a personal assistant who knows almost everything, never gets tired, and can help you with dozens of tasks at once — setting reminders, playing music, answering questions, making calls, checking the weather. That's what a voice assistant provides, for free, built into your devices.

How Does It Understand My Voice?

Several layers of AI work together when you speak to an assistant:

1

Speech Recognition

Your voice is captured and converted into text. The AI has been trained on millions of hours of human speech in many accents, so it understands most people well.

2

Understanding the Meaning

Another AI layer analyses the text and figures out what you actually mean — your intent. "Set a reminder for doctor appointment tomorrow at 10" must be parsed to identify the task (reminder), time (tomorrow 10am), and topic (doctor appointment).

3

Taking Action or Responding

The assistant either performs the action (sets the reminder, plays the song, makes the call) or generates a spoken answer and says it back to you.

The Main Voice Assistants

🍎

Siri

Apple's assistant. Built into iPhones, iPads, Macs, Apple Watch. Say "Hey Siri" to activate. Excellent for Apple device integration — making calls, sending messages, reminders.

🔵

Google Assistant

Built into Android phones and available on iPhones. Say "OK Google" or "Hey Google." Particularly good at answering factual questions and connecting with Google services.

🟠

Amazon Alexa

Lives in Amazon Echo smart speakers (the small round or cylinder-shaped devices). Say "Alexa" to wake it. Excellent for home automation, shopping, and music. Popular in UK homes.

🔷

Cortana / Others

Microsoft's assistant (Windows computers). Less prominent than the others. Various smart TVs and home devices also have built-in voice assistants.

Useful Things to Ask Right Now

Here are some practical commands you can try immediately on whichever assistant you have. Speak naturally — you don't need to use precise wording:

🗣️ Try Saying These

☀️

Weather

"What's the weather like today?" / "Will it rain this weekend?" / "Do I need an umbrella tomorrow?"

⏰

Timers and Reminders

"Set a timer for 20 minutes" / "Remind me to take my tablets at 8pm" / "Set an alarm for 7:30 tomorrow morning"

🎵

Music

"Play some classical music" / "Play Frank Sinatra" / "Play Radio 2"

📞

Calls and Messages (on phone)

"Call Margaret" / "Send a text to John saying I'll be 10 minutes late" / "Read my messages"

❓

General Questions

"What time does Marks and Spencer close?" / "What's the capital of Portugal?" / "How many teaspoons in a tablespoon?"

📺

Entertainment (on smart TV/Alexa)

"Play something on Netflix" / "Turn the TV to BBC One" / "Add milk and bread to my shopping list"

💡

Speak Naturally

You don't need to use stiff, robotic commands. Just speak as you would to a helpful person. "Alexa, I'm making a cup of tea — can you set a timer for 3 minutes?" works just as well as "Alexa, set 3 minute timer."

Smart Speakers at Home

A smart speaker is a small device (often about the size of a tin of beans) that you plug in at home. It's always listening for its "wake word" (like "Alexa" or "Hey Google"), then springs into action when you call it.

The most popular in the UK are the Amazon Echo (uses Alexa) and the Google Nest (uses Google Assistant).

What Smart Speakers Are Particularly Good For

  • Setting timers while your hands are busy in the kitchen — no touching the phone
  • Playing music, radio, or podcasts on request
  • Adding items to a shopping list with your voice
  • Answering quick factual questions
  • Controlling smart home devices — lights, heating, plugs — by voice
  • Making free calls to other Echo devices (useful if a family member also has one)
🏠

Particularly Helpful for Accessibility

For people with arthritis, reduced dexterity, or vision impairment, voice assistants are genuinely transformative. Being able to control the radio, make calls, set reminders, and ask questions entirely by voice — without touching a single button — can make a real difference to independence and daily life.

Privacy and "Always Listening" Devices

A fair concern about smart speakers and voice assistants is: "Is it always listening to me?" The answer needs some nuance.

Smart speakers are designed to listen only for their wake word. The audio is processed locally on the device for the wake word — the rest of your conversations are not sent to Amazon or Google. However, once activated by the wake word, the request is sent to their servers to be processed.

🔐

Sensible Privacy Practices

Don't say sensitive information like bank details, passwords, or deeply personal things near a smart speaker. You can also physically mute the microphone using the button on the device when you want complete privacy. Most smart speaker apps let you review and delete your voice history.

Key Takeaways

  • ✓ Voice assistants (Siri, Alexa, Google) use AI to understand spoken language and respond helpfully
  • ✓ They can set timers, play music, answer questions, make calls, and control smart home devices
  • ✓ Smart speakers are especially helpful for accessibility — controlling things by voice when hands are busy or mobility is limited
  • ✓ Voice assistants wake up only when they hear their trigger word — but be mindful of privacy with sensitive information
  • ✓ Speak naturally — voice assistants are designed to understand normal, conversational language

Quick Check

Two quick questions.

1. You're cooking and want to know how long to boil eggs. Both your hands are covered in flour. What's the quickest solution?

2. Is a smart speaker always sending your conversations to Amazon or Google?
