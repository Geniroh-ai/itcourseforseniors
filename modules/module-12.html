Your First Conversation

Opening a conversation with an AI assistant for the first time is genuinely simple. The interface is a text box — you type, press Enter or click Send, and the AI responds. That's it. No technical knowledge required.

💻 Step-by-Step: Your First Session

1

Choose a tool and open it

Windows: open Copilot from the taskbar · Google: go to gemini.google.com · Any browser: go to claude.ai or chat.openai.com

2

Create a free account (first time only)

Usually just an email address and a password. Takes 2–3 minutes. No credit card needed for the free tier.

3

Click the text box and type your first question

Write naturally. "Can you explain what a pension annuity is in plain English?" is a perfectly good first message. Press Enter.

4

Read the response and follow up

If the answer needs clarifying, ask a follow-up: "Can you give me a specific example?" or "What should I ask my financial adviser about this?" The conversation continues naturally.

5

Start a new conversation when you change topic

Click "New Chat" or the equivalent button when you move to a completely different subject. This keeps things clean and prevents the AI mixing up contexts.

💡

A Perfect Starter Prompt

If you're not sure what to try first, use this: "Hello! I'm just getting started with AI assistants. Can you tell me the 5 most useful things I can ask you to help me with, keeping in mind I'm a retired person looking to make daily life easier?" — it orients the AI to your situation and gives you an immediate, personalised menu of possibilities.

Good Prompts vs. Poor Prompts

The quality of what you get from an AI depends significantly on how you ask. A detailed, context-rich question produces a far more useful answer than a vague one. Here are real-world examples:

✍️ Prompt Comparison — Same Goal, Different Results

❌

"Tell me about my pension"

Too vague. The AI has no context — it will give a generic overview of pensions that may not apply to your situation at all.

✅

"I'm 67 and have a defined benefit pension from my 30 years working for the NHS. I'm considering taking the tax-free lump sum option when I retire in 6 months. Can you explain the key trade-offs I should think about before deciding?"

Specific, contextual, targeted. The AI can give you genuinely relevant, detailed information that directly addresses your situation.

❌

"Write a letter"

To whom? About what? The AI will ask you to clarify — or produce something useless.

✅

"Please write a formal letter of complaint to my energy supplier, Octopus Energy, about a billing error. They charged me £340 for January when I used barely any gas. I want the letter to be polite but firm, and to request a full review of my account. My name is Margaret Willis and my account number is 12345."

Clear recipient, specific issue, exact amounts, desired tone, your details. The AI can produce a near-complete letter you just need to check and send.

The Elements of a Strong Prompt

  • Context about yourself — your age, situation, what you already know, what you're trying to achieve
  • The specific task — exactly what you want produced or explained
  • Relevant details — specific names, dates, numbers, or background that the AI needs to help you properly
  • Format and length — "Give me a bullet-point list" / "Write this as a formal letter" / "Explain it briefly, under 200 words"
  • Tone and audience — "Make it friendly" / "Use technical terms, I understand them" / "Explain as if to someone with no background in this"

Iterating — The Conversation Is the Tool

One of the most powerful things about AI chatbots is that the conversation continues. If the first response isn't quite right, you don't start over — you refine.

🔄 An Iterative Conversation Example

You

"Can you help me write a message to send to my GP surgery requesting an urgent appointment for a persistent cough I've had for 3 weeks?"

AI

Produces a formal message with relevant details included.

You

"That's good but it's a bit long. Can you shorten it to 3 sentences and make it sound less formal — I know the receptionist there."

AI

Produces a shorter, friendlier version.

You

"Perfect. Can you also add that I'm also a bit out of breath when I climb stairs?"

AI

Adds the detail seamlessly to the message.

ℹ️

Useful Refinement Phrases

Keep these in mind for any AI conversation:

  • "Can you make that shorter / longer / simpler / more detailed?"
  • "That's not quite what I meant — what I'm actually looking for is..."
  • "Can you give me three different versions of that?"
  • "Explain that last point a bit more."
  • "What are the risks or downsides I haven't considered?"
  • "Can you summarise that in a single paragraph?"

Real Uses Across Daily Life

Here are detailed, practical examples — showing both the prompt and what kind of response to expect:

🏥 Health and Medical

  • Understanding a diagnosis: "My GP mentioned I have 'moderate mitral valve regurgitation'. Can you explain in plain English what this means, how serious it typically is, and what questions I should ask my cardiologist at my appointment next week?"
  • Medication information: "I've just been prescribed amlodipine 5mg for blood pressure. Can you explain what it does, what side effects I might notice in the first few weeks, and whether there are any foods or drinks I should avoid?" (Always confirm with your pharmacist.)
  • Preparing for an appointment: "I have a 10-minute GP appointment tomorrow to discuss my fatigue and joint pain. Can you help me prepare a clear, brief summary of my symptoms and suggest the questions most worth asking, so I don't forget anything under pressure?"

📝 Writing and Administration

  • Formal complaints: "I want to complain to my local council about a pothole on my street that has been there for 4 months and I've reported twice. Please write a firm but polite formal letter making clear I expect action within 14 days and will escalate to my councillor if not resolved."
  • Benefit appeals: "My Personal Independence Payment (PIP) claim was rejected. The reason given was that they assessed my mobility as being higher than it is. Help me draft an appeal letter that clearly explains how my condition affects my daily mobility."
  • Understanding documents: "Here is an extract from my insurance policy document [paste text]. Can you explain in plain English what I'm actually covered for, and highlight anything that might be an important exclusion I should know about?"

💰 Finance and Benefits

  • Understanding entitlements: "I'm 68, retired, and receive the State Pension. My husband passed away last year and he was also receiving the State Pension. Can you explain what benefits or pension changes I might be entitled to as a surviving spouse?"
  • Explaining financial jargon: "My bank has sent me a letter about 'redeeming my bond at maturity' and 'rollover options'. Can you explain what this means in plain English and what the main choices I'll need to make are?"

🏡 Home and Practical

  • DIY guidance: "The silicone seal around my bath has gone black with mould and is peeling away. Can you walk me through how to remove it and apply new silicone sealant step by step, including what tools and materials I need and any precautions to take?"
  • Appliance troubleshooting: "My Bosch washing machine (Series 4, about 5 years old) is making a loud grinding noise during the spin cycle but still seems to wash clothes normally. What are the most likely causes and which ones can I fix myself vs. needing an engineer?"

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Family and Social

  • Explaining things to grandchildren: "My 8-year-old granddaughter asked me why the sky is blue. I understand it has something to do with light scattering. Can you give me an accurate but child-friendly explanation I can use to explain it to her, and maybe a simple demonstration we could do together?"
  • Speech writing: "I've been asked to give a short 3-minute speech at my daughter's 50th birthday party next month. She's a nurse, loves gardening, and has a great sense of humour. I want the speech to be warm, funny, and end on something meaningful. Can you draft something for me to adapt?"

A Framework for When to Use AI

✅ High-Value, Lower-Risk Uses

These are areas where AI is genuinely excellent and where errors are easy to spot or low-stakes:

  • Writing first drafts (letters, emails, speeches) — you review and edit before sending
  • Explaining concepts and jargon — verify key facts but the explanation itself is low-risk
  • Brainstorming ideas — you evaluate the suggestions yourself
  • Summarising or simplifying long documents you have in front of you
  • Planning (trips, events, schedules) — treat the output as a starting point
  • Translation — check critical translations with a native speaker or professional
  • Learning and exploration — reading about topics, satisfying curiosity

⚠️ Use with Care — Verify Before Acting

Useful for understanding and preparing, but always verify with authoritative sources or professionals:

  • Medical — excellent for understanding diagnoses, medications, and preparing questions. Never replace a GP or specialist with AI. Don't use AI to self-diagnose or decide on treatment.
  • Legal — useful for understanding legal concepts and processes, but qualified solicitors are irreplaceable for actual legal decisions, contracts, and disputes.
  • Financial — helpful for understanding products and terminology. For actual investment, pension, and tax decisions, use a qualified financial adviser.
  • Recent events and current information — AI's knowledge has a cut-off date. For anything time-sensitive, check official or news sources.

Critical Thinking with AI Output

Developing a habit of evaluating AI output critically is one of the most valuable skills you can build. Here's a practical checklist:

  • Is this a verifiable fact or a general explanation? General explanations (how the heart works) are low risk. Specific facts (a medication dosage, a legal deadline) need verification.
  • Does the AI acknowledge uncertainty? A well-designed AI will often say "I believe" or "you should verify this" on topics where it's not confident. Take those caveats seriously.
  • Does it feel plausible given what you already know? Your life experience is valuable. If something the AI says contradicts what you know from years of experience, push back.
  • Have I asked about the other side? Try: "What are the arguments against this?" or "What am I missing?" to get a more balanced picture.
  • For decisions with real consequences — who am I checking this with? Before acting on AI advice for health, legal, or financial matters, run it past the appropriate professional.
🧐

Confident ≠ Correct

AI responses are written in confident, fluent prose. This is a feature of how they generate text — it says nothing about accuracy. A hallucinated book title is stated in exactly the same tone as an accurate one. The confidence in the presentation is not a signal of reliability. Your judgement and verification remain essential.

Privacy — What to Share and What Not To

When you type something into an AI chatbot, that text is typically sent to the company's servers for processing. Here is a sensible set of privacy practices:

✅

Generally Fine to Share

General medical conditions (not specific test results). First names only. General financial situations without account numbers. Publicly available information. Topics you're learning about.

⚠️

Avoid Sharing

Full name + address + date of birth together. Bank account or credit card numbers. NHS number or National Insurance number. Passwords or security question answers. Other people's private information without their consent.

🔐

Using AI Professionally

If you're using AI for anything involving a business or organisation, check their data policy first. Many companies now have AI tools built into workplace software (e.g., Microsoft 365 Copilot), where data stays within the organisation. For personal use on free consumer tools, be conscious of what personal details you share.

Key Takeaways

  • ✓ Start with a free account on any major AI tool — the interface is as simple as texting
  • ✓ Context-rich prompts (your situation, specific details, desired format) produce dramatically better results
  • ✓ The conversation is the tool — refine, follow up, and iterate until you have what you need
  • ✓ AI is excellent for writing, understanding, planning, and learning — verify before acting on anything important
  • ✓ Your judgement is the final check — confident AI output is not the same as accurate AI output
  • ✓ Don't share sensitive personal identifiers (account numbers, NI numbers) in AI conversations
