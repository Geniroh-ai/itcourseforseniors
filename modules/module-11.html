Large Language Models — Chatbots Explained

What is ChatGPT, really? How does it understand you and write back? Here's the clearest explanation you'll find anywhere.

🟣 AI Deep Dive ⏱ 15 minutes 📖 Reading

What You'll Learn

  • What a "Large Language Model" (LLM) is — in plain, jargon-free terms
  • How ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude work at a strategic level
  • How these systems seem to "understand" language without truly thinking
  • The remarkable things LLMs can do — and their clear limitations
  • Why this technology is considered genuinely revolutionary

A New Kind of AI Has Arrived

If you've heard of ChatGPT, you've heard of a Large Language Model (or LLM). In late 2022, ChatGPT was released by a company called OpenAI, and the world's reaction was one of stunned amazement. Millions of people tried it and found an AI that could hold a natural conversation, write essays, explain complex topics simply, help with letters, answer medical questions, and much more.

Since then, several major LLMs have arrived: Gemini from Google, Claude from Anthropic, Copilot from Microsoft, and others. They are all broadly similar in what they do.

Understanding what these tools are — and what they aren't — is one of the most valuable things you can learn right now, because they are becoming part of everyday life very quickly.

What Does "Large Language Model" Mean?

Let's break down those three words:

📖 Breaking Down the Name

📦

"Large"

These models are enormous. ChatGPT was trained on hundreds of billions of words of text. The "model" itself — the set of learned connections — has hundreds of billions of parameters (mathematical values that were adjusted during training). This is what requires massive computing power to create.

🗣️

"Language"

The model specialises in language — reading text, understanding it, and producing new text in response. It works with human language (words, sentences, meaning) rather than, say, images or audio primarily.

🧮

"Model"

A "model" in AI is the trained system — a mathematical structure that has learned from data. It's like a very complex, distilled summary of everything it read during training, stored as billions of numbers.

How Does It Actually Work?

You don't need to understand the mathematics, but the core idea is surprisingly accessible. An LLM was trained by reading an enormous amount of text — books, articles, websites, Wikipedia, academic papers — and learning to predict what word comes next in a sentence.

📖

The "Next Word" Prediction Game

Imagine you've read a million books. Someone says to you: "It was a dark and stormy ___." You'd almost certainly say "night." Not because you looked it up — but because you've absorbed enough language to recognise the pattern. LLMs do this at massive scale. Given your message, they predict the most natural and sensible response, word by word, billions of times a day.

But "predicting the next word" sounds too simple to explain a system that can write poetry, explain quantum physics, and help with your tax return. Here's why it works so well:

  • To predict language well, you have to understand a lot — grammar, facts, logic, context, tone
  • Training on enormous amounts of text forces the model to develop a rich internal representation of knowledge
  • The result is a model that appears to "understand" — not because it truly thinks, but because it has been exposed to so much human expression that it reflects it back remarkably well
🤔

Does It Actually "Understand"?

This is one of the great debates in AI. LLMs don't understand in the way humans do — they have no consciousness, no feelings, no real-world experience. But they process language with such sophistication that the outputs are often indistinguishable from genuine understanding. The key thing for you to know: they are extremely useful, even if the "understanding" is different from ours.

What These Tools Are Remarkably Good At

The capabilities of modern LLMs are genuinely impressive. Here's a practical overview of what they excel at:

✍️

Writing and Editing

Drafting letters, emails, cards, complaints, formal requests. Proofreading and improving what you've already written. Writing in different styles — formal, friendly, humorous.

🔍

Explaining Complex Things Simply

Ask "Can you explain my pension statement in plain English?" or "What does this medical letter mean?" — and receive a clear, jargon-free explanation tailored to your level.

💡

Answering Questions

General knowledge, historical facts, recipes, how-to guides, explanations of news events, comparisons of products. A knowledgeable companion on almost any topic.

🌍

Translation

Translate text to and from dozens of languages — useful for reading foreign menus, understanding documents, or writing to someone abroad.

📋

Summarising

Paste in a long document and ask for a brief summary. Extract the key points from a lengthy article, report, or letter.

💬

Conversation and Brainstorming

Talk through a problem, get advice, brainstorm ideas, plan a holiday, plan a garden — the conversation flows naturally, and the AI asks follow-up questions to help you further.

Clear Limitations to Know About

To use LLMs wisely, you need to understand where they fall short. These are not minor quibbles — they're important:

!

They Can Confidently State Wrong Information

This is the most important limitation. LLMs sometimes generate incorrect facts stated as if they were true. Always verify important information — especially for medical, legal, financial, or historical facts.

!

Knowledge Has a Cut-Off Date

LLMs are trained up to a certain date. They don't know about events after that point unless the system has been updated with web browsing (some do).

⚠️

No Real-Time Information

Without web access, they can't check today's news, stock prices, sports results, or live service information.

⚠️

No Memory Between Conversations

Each new conversation typically starts fresh. The AI doesn't remember what you talked about last week unless you tell it again.

⚠️

Not a Professional Substitute

They can provide useful general information, but they are not your doctor, solicitor, or financial adviser. Use them to understand and prepare for conversations with real professionals — not to replace them.

The Main Tools You Might Encounter

🟢

ChatGPT

Made by OpenAI. The most widely known. Free version available at chat.openai.com. Excellent general capabilities. Paid version (ChatGPT Plus) adds more features.

💎

Gemini

Made by Google. Built into Google Search and available at gemini.google.com. Integrates well with Gmail and Google Docs. Good for tasks alongside Google services.

🔶

Claude

Made by Anthropic. Available at claude.ai. Known for being thoughtful, careful, and good at longer documents. Free tier available.

🪟

Copilot

Made by Microsoft (powered by OpenAI technology). Built into Windows 11, Microsoft 365, and Bing search. Very accessible if you use Windows computers.

🚀

Where to Start

If you're on a Windows computer, try Microsoft Copilot — it's built in and well-supported. If you use Google for everything, try Gemini. For iPhone users, the next version of Siri is deeply powered by LLM technology. All are excellent starting points.

Why This Feels Like a Turning Point

People who follow technology closely describe LLMs as the most significant technological development since the invention of the internet — and possibly much more significant than that. Here's why:

  • Accessible to everyone — no technical skills needed. If you can type or speak, you can use it
  • Works across nearly every domain — law, medicine, education, creative writing, coding, customer service, research
  • Improving rapidly — each new version is substantially more capable than the last
  • Changing how work is done — many professions are already using LLMs as tools that multiply their effectiveness

You don't need to know how they work under the bonnet to benefit from them. In the final module, we'll walk through exactly how you can use them in your daily life.

Key Takeaways

  • ✓ A Large Language Model (LLM) is an AI trained on vast amounts of text that can converse, write, explain, and assist
  • ✓ They work by predicting the most natural next word — at enormous scale and sophistication
  • ✓ They are excellent at writing, explaining, summarising, translating, and answering questions
  • ✓ They can be confidently wrong — always verify important factual claims
  • ✓ ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and Copilot are the main tools — all have free versions you can try
